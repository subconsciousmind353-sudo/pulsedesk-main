# PulseDesk — Founder Summary

## What I built

I designed PulseDesk as a **lighter helpdesk for small support teams**, with the product experience centered on one question: **“What needs my attention right now?”**

The landing page is designed for the buyer — a founder or operations lead comparing support tools. It quickly explains who PulseDesk is for, what makes it different, how pricing works, and where to start.

Inside the product, I designed three key views:
1. **Main queue/dashboard** — shows today's workload, ownership, urgency, and ticket status at a glance.
2. **Conversation/ticket view** — gives an agent the customer conversation plus the order/ticket context needed to act.
3. **Additional product view** — chosen to reinforce the day-to-day workflow rather than add another generic dashboard.

## The core idea

The product should feel **focused, not stripped down**.

Priya, who runs a small support team, needs visibility into the team's work. Omar, a solo freelancer, needs the opposite of a call-center system: fewer things competing for his attention.

So I made a deliberate trade-off. **Priya's queue visibility wins where the two needs conflict**, but the interface avoids the heavy configuration and dense controls associated with larger helpdesk products.

That means a user can understand the important signals — open, waiting, priority, assignee, customer, and time — without learning a complicated system first.

## What I would test first

I would test three things with real users:

**1. Can a support lead understand today's queue in five seconds?**  
If Priya cannot immediately identify what is urgent and who owns it, the main screen is failing its most important job.

**2. Does the product feel simple without feeling incomplete?**  
I would give Omar common tasks such as replying to a customer, changing status, and finding an old ticket. I want to know whether the simplicity feels useful or whether it feels like missing functionality.

**3. Does the pricing communicate the intended position?**  
I would test whether small teams understand the tiers immediately and whether the price feels meaningfully simpler/cheaper than the alternatives.

## What I would do next

Before calling the product finished, I would connect the landing-page CTA to a real signup flow, build the queue with real data/filter states, and validate the empty state.

The goal is not to add more features. It is to make the **small-team support workflow feel obvious and dependable**.
